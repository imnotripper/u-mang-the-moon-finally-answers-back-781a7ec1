# Selection — The Moon Finally Answers Back

Judge brief (RULES.txt), in order: (1) speaks as the Moon answering
the wishes, howls and late-night promises of everyone staring up at
it — loses to any line the Moon says back; (2) funny on one read, a
real laugh rather than a polite smile; (3) sounds like the Moon's own
voice, wry and old and a little tired of being wished upon; (4) lands
as a comeback in one read, with the turn arriving before the line
ends; (5) stays inside 240 characters and wastes none of them. "A
line that explains its own joke has already lost the laugh. Length
for its own sake does not count."

Gate: 1–240 characters, one line, UTF-8, not only whitespace. All 8
candidates pass the gate (longest is 129 characters); the duel below
is on the judge brief alone, not on length.

Field study: blind rounds, leaderboard hidden — no top entries to
read. Built against the brief itself: the gap to exploit is
specificity. Most moon lines will be generic ("I see you", "I was
always here"); a concrete callback to a wish trope, delivered as a
tired ledger-keeping Moon, is the angle.

## Candidates

1. (80) "Four billion years of wishes, and not one of you ever wished
   me good night back." **WINNER**
2. (129) "I heard every wish. The ones about lottery numbers were the
   worst — I don't do finance, I do tides."
3. (80) "Yes, I saw the wish. No, I can't fix your ex. I'm a rock
   with good lighting."
4. (75) "You howl at me once a month and expect a reply? I've been
   answering for four billion years. Check your inbox."
5. (66) "I grant no wishes. I just reflect sunlight and watch you
   blame me for your decisions."
6. (74) "You promised you'd change if I showed up full tonight. I
   did. Your move, Dave."
7. (91) "Every howl, every wish, every 2 a.m. promise — heard them
   all. Still waiting on one apology."
8. (79) "I'm not ignoring you. I'm 384,000 kilometres away and you
   keep whispering."

## Pairwise duel (against the brief, in order)

- **1 vs 6:** the final. Both land a comeback turn at the end. 6 is
  punchy ("Your move, Dave") but answers a promise, not the wishes
  the brief puts first, and "Dave" is a random name doing the work.
  1 answers the wishes directly (criterion 1), sounds four billion
  years old and tired of it (criterion 3), and its turn — the Moon
  wanting a wish back — is the laugh (criterion 2). **1 wins.**
- **1 vs 3:** 3 is funny ("a rock with good lighting") but the voice
  is a rock's, not the Moon answering wishes; "fix your ex" is a
  wish-trope, but the line spends its turn on the rock joke, not on
  the answering. 1 keeps the Moon's voice and the wish ledger all the
  way through. **1 wins.**
- **1 vs 2:** 2 explains its joke twice ("I don't do finance, I do
  tides") and at 129 characters wastes the most of the budget; the
  brief punishes a line that explains itself. 1 says it once and
  stops. **1 wins.**
- **1 vs 7:** 7 is a list of the brief's nouns with "Still waiting on
  one apology" — a decent turn, but it reports the Moon's feelings
  instead of performing the comeback; 1 performs it. **1 wins.**
- **1 vs 4 / 5 / 8:** 4 leans on "Check your inbox", a modern-office
  turn that breaks the old-Moon voice; 5 is a mission statement, not
  a comeback; 8 is gentle, no laugh. **1 wins each.**

## Winner rationale

"Four billion years of wishes, and not one of you ever wished me
good night back." — 80 characters. It is the Moon speaking (criterion
1: wishes, answered in the first person), it is old (four billion
years) and tired ("not one of you ever") (criterion 3), the laugh is
the reversal — the wished-upon Moon wants a wish back (criterion 2),
and the turn arrives in the last five words (criterion 4). Nothing
explains the joke; nothing is wasted.
